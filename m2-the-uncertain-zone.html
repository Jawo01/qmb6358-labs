The Uncertain Zone

A software company will only enter a new market if its share beats 16%. Please run the market panel yourself (many times) to see what "95% confident" really means, and what to do when the interval straddles the line.

This lab takes about 15 minutes and also works on a phone. Nothing is graded or saved.

1Run one market panel

This is the book's market-share case §3.10.3 · p. 130. Finance has set a break-even rule, i.e. the first-year share must exceed 16%. A market panel records 1,000 actual purchases in the target market, and the share is the fraction of purchases that chose our brand. Each click runs a new panel.

A 95% interval comes from a method that, when repeated, catches the true value about 95% of the time. In other words, about 5 in 100 intervals miss, even when everything is done right §3.4.2 · p. 105.

3Add the break-even line

Next, we read each panel's interval against the 16% break-even line. An interval entirely above 16% is a clear go, one entirely below is a clear no-go, and one that crosses 16% falls in the uncertain zone §3.9.3 · p. 124.

4The real panel: 14.1% to 18.7%

The book reports a share of 16.4% in 1,000 purchases. The 95% interval runs from 14.1% to 18.7%, and the one-sided 95% lower bound is 14.5% §3.10.3 · p. 131.

In the uncertain zone, the book's playbook is to collect more data, commit in stages, negotiate, or accept the risk explicitly and document it. What it rules out is presenting a near-threshold estimate as certain §3.9.3 · p. 124.
