Same Data, Different Story

Every chart on a dashboard rests on design choices, for instance where the axis starts or how wide the bins are. In this lab, the data stay the same throughout, and only these choices change. Please watch, at each step, what a viewer would conclude.

This lab takes about 15 minutes and also works on a phone. Nothing is graded or saved.

1Where the axis starts

The lab uses 515 house sales in a synthetic town modeled on the Kaggle data, with eight invented neighborhoods. The chart shows the median sale price in each neighborhood. Please change where the value axis starts.

2How wide the bins are

A histogram groups the prices into ranges (bins) and counts the sales in each range §2.4.3 · p. 37. Tableau, R Commander and AI tools all choose a bin width for you unless you set one. Please move the slider.

3Mean or median, and in which order

Riverside is a neighborhood of modest homes plus 5 lakefront homes that sold for more than half a million dollars each. Please switch between the median and the mean, and between alphabetical and sorted order.

Summary
Order

The book’s design principles include sorting: if categories are not sorted, the reader wastes time searching for what matters §2.4.7 · p. 44.

4Totals, averages and counts

Tableau aggregates a measure when you drag it onto a chart, and its default aggregation is SUM, so a chart of sale prices by neighborhood often shows totals. Please compare the three versions.

Show by neighborhood

5The color scale

Dashboards often show neighborhoods as colored tiles or on a map. Here each tile is one neighborhood, colored by its median sale price. Please try the three color scales, i.e. shades that start at $0, shades that run from the cheapest to the most expensive neighborhood (min to max), and red and green around the town median.
