QMB 6358 · Module 1 Decision Lab · after Chapter 1

From Question to Decision

An AI tool will answer any question you ask, including a vague one. In this lab, you improve one vague request step by step, compare the AI’s answers, and decide which change made the biggest difference.

This lab takes about 15 minutes and also works on a phone. Nothing is graded or saved. There is no live AI in the lab: the answers are pre-written examples of what an AI tool typically returns, and I computed every number in them from the course file HousePrices.csv.

2Say what you need to decide

According to the book, a strong prompt first states the decision context, i.e. what is at stake and what you are trying to decide §1.4.4 · p. 10. Novikov calls this the “why” and the goal of a project, which should come before any data work Novikov · pp. 10–11. The new part of the prompt is highlighted.

3Ask for a fair comparison

A good request also names the comparisons that matter §2.11.2 · p. 82. For a list price, that means homes like your client’s, since a group of homes can look expensive or cheap simply because of what it is made of §2.6.1 · p. 53.

4State a hypothesis and ask for a breakdown

The book’s example of a good prompt asks the AI to separate a change into its parts (in that example, volume versus average order value) §1.4.5 · p. 11. Here the natural parts of a price gap are home size and the price per square foot. In addition, Novikov suggests stating a hypothesis first, i.e. a testable statement of what you believe is happening Novikov · p. 12.

5Ask how sure the answer is

The last part of the book’s good prompt asks for the most plausible explanations and for the additional data that would reduce the uncertainty §1.4.5 · p. 11. In the book’s own words, “The result is not instant certainty. The result is faster clarity about what is plausible and what is not” §1.4.3 · p. 10.

6Write your own prompt

If you are working on the discussion M0 The AI Whisperer Challenge, please use the thing that you want to predict there; otherwise, please use a decision from your work. Fill in the five boxes and copy the prompt into your AI tool. The structure follows the book’s fill-in-the-blank template for exploration requests §2.11.4 · p. 86. Please do not enter any personal or confidential data (for instance, names, addresses or your employer’s numbers).

Then check the answer

  • Does the answer restate your decision, and does every part of it help with that decision?
  • Does it compare like with like, or does it lump together cases that differ (as the first house answer did with the three neighborhoods)?
  • Does it give a range and say which data would narrow it? Moreover, does it present any correlation as a cause? Detecting statements like that is one of the tasks that remain yours §1.4.6 · p. 11.
